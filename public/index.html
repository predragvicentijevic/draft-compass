THE RESEARCH, EXPLAINED

How we rank your picks

The app combines the manually researched In lane and In game relationships on dotacounterpick’s hero pages with the explicitly hand-picked Our counter picks section on DotaFuel’s counter pages. Statistical matchup tables, win rates, match counts, advantages, and Draft Helper data are excluded. You can use either source separately or both.

A transparent matchup score

Counter tiers become points: Best 4 · Strong 3 · Good 2 · Mediocre 1. Untiered entries receive 1 point. In-game points count fully; lane points count half because the enemy’s lane is unknown. These weights are our ranking method.

For each enemy, add the points when your hero counters them and subtract the points when they counter your hero. If the same pair appears more than once in a phase, only the strongest entry counts. Group members use the group’s tier.

Sum across the selected enemies, then rank all eligible heroes. Ties use favorable matchups, fewer unfavorable matchups, then hero name. A zero with no documented evidence means unknown, not a proven even matchup. Scores are not win probabilities.

Combining sources

DotaFuel’s hand-picked recommendations have no strength tiers or lane/game labels. We treat each as an untiered general in-game counter worth 1 point; their display order and statistics never affect scores. For the same directed matchup in the same phase, only the strongest entry across the selected sources scores. Agreement is shown as evidence from both sources, with no duplicate point bonus. References retain their source and ability names, with links to the complete original explanations. Role pools continue to follow dotacounterpick’s labels.

Worst case scenario

This optional mode works with two or four enemies selected. With two selected enemies, it adds the three most negative remaining net matchups to each recommendation’s current score. With four selected enemies, it adds the single most negative remaining net matchup. The hypothetical enemies are distinct and exclude already selected enemies and the recommended hero itself.

Each recommendation has its own hypothetical enemy lineup. All enemy roles are considered because their remaining roles are unknown. Existing in-game and lane weights apply in both directions. Tied matchups are ordered alphabetically; only the number of open enemy slots is counted, and equally bad alternatives at the cutoff are shown separately. If fewer documented negative matchups exist than open slots, the rest add 0; missing evidence is unknown.

Expand a recommendation to see its original score, each hypothetical enemy, the individual adjustments, and ability evidence. Unchecking restores the normal ranking. The option resets and disables when the enemy count is not two or four.

Roles & ability evidence

Role pools match the labels on the source’s hero pages. Position 4 and 5 share its Support pool. Ability names on expanded recommendations come from the research’s linked ability references. Read the linked source for the complete explanation and any lane or build conditions.
